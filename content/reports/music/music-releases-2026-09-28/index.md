## Executive Summary

A transitional week where the year's strongest **noise-rock** release — **Chat Pile**'s
*Who Loves the Sun* (out Sept 4 on The Flenser) — is still generating press, and the
imminent **Oct 2** New-Music-Friday brings a heavy, on-taste post-punk slate. The flagship

## 1. Chat Pile — *Who Loves the Sun* (out Sept 4, The Flenser)

The standout for your **noise-rock / sludge** lane — and the single most essential watchlist
release so far this season. The Oklahoma four-piece (frontman Raygun Busch) followed their
2022 breakthrough *God's Country* with *Who Loves the Sun* on **September 4** via **The Flenser**,
and the critical response has been enormous and unusually split — which is exactly what you
want from a band this good. Pitchfork, The Guardian, Paste and Exclaim! weighed in around Sept 4;
the *Guardian* called it "sludge metallers resurface with a slightly limiting alt-rock rebrand,"
while *The Needle Drop*, *Paste* and *Revolver*-adjacent outlets heard a bleaker, more
uncompromising record. *Mother Jones* ran a long feature ("It's the End of the World as Chat Pile
Knows It," Sept 18), and *Louder Than War*'s review came **Sept 26** — meaning this is still the
record people are chewing on this very week. Singles "PEN I S MALL" and "Same Rules" gave the
best previews of its slow-motion-apocalypse mood. It's the flagship member of the watchlist's
noise-rock wing out this year. *Status:* Out now. *Sources:* The Flenser, Pitchfork, The Guardian,
Paste, Exclaim!, Mother Jones, Louder Than War, Invisible Oranges (Aug 30 – Sept 26 2026).

## 2. The Fall — *Post Script* (final album, out this week / Oct 2)

The year's most consequential **post-punk** event — albeit a complicated one. *Post Script*,
dubbed the "definitive final studio album" of Britain's most riotously idiosyncratic band,
collects **nine previously unreleased tracks** from The Fall's later era, bristling with Mark E.
Smith's caustic wit, hypnotic repetition and jagged post-punk energy. It's slated for the
**Sept 28 – Oct 4** window (the Oct 2 New Music Friday). But the backstory is doing the rounds:
Smith's **fiancée and last manager publicly condemned the release as a "cash grab"** and
"ethically wrong," while colleagues insist they hold his *actual* last recordings (including a
cover of Madonna's "Material Girl," per MusicRadar). The Guardian framed it as "love and respect
or a cash grab? The furious battle over the final album by the Fall." For anyone with Have a Nice
Life–to-Home Front post-punk on the playlist, this is essential listening either way — the last
record by a band that shaped five decades of outsider rock. *Status:* Out Oct 2 (this week).
*Sources:* Tinnitist, The Guardian, NME, Paste, MusicRadar, BrooklynVegan (Jun–Sep 2026).

## 3. Swans — *Newly Sentient Being* (out Oct 2)

For the **art-punk / post-punk / noise** extremes of the list, this is a must-hear. *Newly
Sentient Being* is **Swans**' final statement from their "Big Sound" era — superb recordings of
the band's last concert of that incarnation, recorded **Nov 28, 2025, in Berlin**, with more than
**two hours of music**, a wealth of material never recorded elsewhere, and a DVD of the
performance. Michael Gira is explicit that this closes the chapter ("this phase is finished"),
with his next Swans turn promised as "something decidedly more humble and intimate." Coming on
Oct 2, it captures the full final lineup (Norman Westberg, Dana Schechter, Phil Puleo, Larry
Mullins) at what Gira calls "a particularly high note." An essential marker for the 
slow-burn, towering side of your post-rock / post-punk taste. *Status:* Out Oct 2.
*Sources:* Tinnitist (label press release via Mute/Young God), MusicBrainz (2026-10-02).

## 4. Deerhoof — *Divine Schism* (out Oct 2)

The evergreen San Francisco art-noise four-piece return with *Divine Schism*, billed as a
"**sci-fi oratorio**" — matching some of their rawest crust-core to the richest orchestral
textures of any Deerhoof record, telling the story of the Earth from the Sun's perspective.
Recorded partly at home and at Russian Recording with Mike Bridavsky, it layers twin-killer
guitars, untamed drums and Satomi's imperturbable voice into something "quite unlike anything
they've ever come up with." This is squarely your **noise-rock / art-punk** lane — the band
nearest the creative-ferocity end of the playlist. *Status:* Out Oct 2.
*Sources:* Tinnitist (label press release), MusicBrainz (2026-10-02).

## 5. Denzel Curry & Kenneth Blume — *ii* (out Aug 21)

The **rap / hip-hop** pick, and a genuine watchlist crossover: **Denzel Curry** reunited with
**Kenneth Blume** (formerly Kenny Beats) for *ii*, produced by Blume. Reviews ran heavily the week
of Aug 20–28 — Pitchfork, Everything Is Noise, treblezine, Clash — and it arrives on the heels of
the Yebba-featuring lead single "DIFFERENCE" (announced Aug 12). It's a rangey, hyper-produced
returns-to-form from a rapper already on your list. If the playlist's rap lane is underserved
these days, this is the record to add. *Status:* Out Aug 21 (recent). *Sources:* Pitchfork,
Stereogum, Consequence, BrooklynVegan, treblezine (Aug 12–28 2026), MusicBrainz (2026-08-21).
