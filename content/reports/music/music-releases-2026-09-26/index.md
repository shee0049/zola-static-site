## 1. Protomartyr — *Hotel Usona* (out Friday, Sept 25, Domino)

The big **new-music-Friday** arrival for your post-punk side. Detroit's
**Protomartyr** — the art-punk/post-punk quartet fronted by the keenly pessimistic
**Joe Casey** — released *Hotel Usona* on **Sept 25** via **Domino Records**,
with critics calling it a career-high pinnacle. It caps a roughly **16-year
journey** (the band formed in the late 2000s as a hardcore side-project) and
lands as the follow-up to 2023's *Formal Growth in Attitude*. The record was
built around Casey's meditation on "Usona" — the American house-wife archetype
from Frank Lloyd Wright's "Usonia" project — and packages a suite of anxious,
wryly funny, post-punk vignettes. Two late singles framed the release:
**"Sounds We Cannot Hear"** (July) and **"Exalted Eagles"** (Sept 21, with a
video from Domino). *Paste*, *Exclaim!* and *The Line of Best Fit* all reviewed
it this week. If your *Formal Growth in Attitude* or *Ultimate Success Today*
records still get regular spins, this is the week's must-check. *Status:* Out
Friday, Sept 25. *Sources:* Domino Records, Paste, Exclaim!, The Line of Best
Fit, Northern Transmissions (Sept 2026).

## 2. Chat Pile — *Who Loves the Sun* (out Sept 4, The Flenser)

The most on-target **noise-rock** release in your lane this month. **Chat Pile**
— the Oklahoma noise-rock four-piece whose blown-out, pummeling noise-rock has made
them one of the genre's defining current voices — put out *Who Loves the Sun*
on **September 4** via **The Flenser**, their first album since the acclaimed
2022 breakthrough *God's Country* and 2024's *Cool World*. It arrived with a
strong press run (Pitchfork, New Noise, The Needle Drop, Lambgoat, and a fresh
Louder Than War review this morning), and the label billed it as the band
"staring into the abyss" — as grim, rhythmic and sludgy as their best work.
With The Flenser releasing everything from Have a Nice Life to Drowse, this sits
at the very center of your noise/sludge taste. If you've been waiting on a new
Chat Pile, this is it. *Status:* Out now (released Sept 4). *Sources:* The
Flenser, Pitchfork, New Noise Magazine, Lambgoat, Louder Than War (Aug–Sept
2026).

## 3. Sleep — *Hempispheres* (out Sept 3, Third Man)

A genuine **sludge/doom legend return**. **Sleep** — the San Jose stoner-doom
titans behind *Dopesmoker* and *Holy Mountain* — are back with **Hempispheres**,
released on **September 3** via **Third Man Records**. It's a notable one-man
turn: reviews (StereoGum-adjacent outlets, *invisibleoranges*) describe it as
**Al Cisneros** carrying a comeback album essentially solo — "a one-man
reunion" — following the 2018 *The Sciences* and a long period of the
classic-line-up being on indefinite pause. Announced back in **August** (first
single **"The Morrisist"**, preceded by the June track **"Have Spacesuit Will
Travel"**), the 38-minute record keeps the signature slow, riff-heavy, cannabis-
cosmic crush that Windhand and Kowloon Walled City fans will recognize instantly.
Not technically a brand-new band for the list, but a heavyweight addition to the
sludge flank that's been shaping this month. *Status:* Out now (released Sept
3). *Sources:* Third Man Records, Revolver, Ghost Cult, invisibleoranges,
Paste (Aug–Sept 2026).
