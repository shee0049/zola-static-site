1. Doom Scroll — Exhausted Dialogue (out today, Oct 6, Flail Records)

The day's freshest on-lane full-length, and the headliner for the indie/folk-punk stretch. Colorado's Doom Scroll release Exhausted Dialogue today, October 6 via Flail Records — an eleven-track set exploring the state of the world, war, curses, mental health and loss. Punknews premiered the record this morning with a vocalist/multi-instrumentalist Elliot Lozier track-by-track breakdown, running from the film-score-cued opener "Awakening 2" through songs like "Panopticon" and "Rapid Eyes." It's literate, wordy, genre-mutant folk-punk with a strong DIY lineage — squarely in the Jeff Rosenstock / Front Porch Step neighborhood of your list. Status: Out today, Oct 6. Sources: Punknews.org (Oct 6), MusicBrainz (2026-10-06).

2. Carly Rae Jepsen — Day and Night (Continued) (out Oct 9)

A tidy Canadian indie-pop flag for the coming Friday. Carly Rae Jepsen announced Day and Night (Continued), an eight-song B-sides companion to this year's double album Day and Night, arriving October 9. As she's done for almost every record, the first four tracks live in the Day universe and the latter four continue the Night realm — tracklist: "Reaching for a Star," "Sixteen and Rising," "Wedding Season," "Lullaby," "Yes," "Muscle Memory," "Love Depressed" and "In Pure Faith." Announced via Instagram and picked up by Exclaim! (Oct 5), Clash, Rolling Stone and Pitchfork the same day — a dependable, instantly-listenable listen for the wider Canadian/indie corner, even if it's pop rather than the heavier end of your list. Status: Out Oct 9. Sources: Exclaim! (Oct 5), Rolling Stone (Oct 5).

3. L7 — "Loma Linda" + Last Hurrah tour (single out now; tour starts Oct 6)

A grunge/classic-acts item with real weight behind it. L7 released a new song, "Loma Linda," this week — and it's a poignant one. The demo was originally written in 2003 with then-bassist Janis Tanaka (in the band 1999–2001), and the song was completed this month as the LA grunge icons gear up for their final North American run, the "Last Hurrah" tour, which begins October 6. Tanaka is rejoining on bass following the death of founding bassist Jennifer Finch earlier this year. Punknews and Rolling Stone both flagged the track (Rolling Stone noting the Janis Tanaka 2003 origin). For the Bricks Are Heavy-era crowd this is one of the week's most emotionally loaded drop — a fitting rock-solid last-chapter single. Status: Single out now; "Last Hurrah" tour starts Oct 6. Sources: Punknews.org (Oct 5), Rolling Stone (Oct 5), Blabbermouth (Oct 5).

4. 72 Rats — it all burns down someday (out Oct 9)

A reasonably under-the-radar post-punk/new-wave pickup. Seattle-based indie-rock project 72 Rats — known for thoughtful, concept-minded records like Nothing Left to Lose — release their new album it all burns down someday on the major streaming platforms October 9, with CD, cassette, Bandcamp and Subvert physicals already available since October 2. It's a collection of six new songs ("Pull Me Back," "Ready To Roll," etc.) built on a poetic, acceptance-themed lyric core — a testament that "everything ends one day, but that shouldn't stop you from living fully." Milder and more melodic than the noise/sludge end of your taste, but a clean, vocal-forward post-punk/indie entry for the Viagra Boys / Home Front-adjacent stretch. Status: Streaming Oct 9; physicals out now. Sources: Daily Music Roll (Oct 5).

5. Death Cassette — "Clutter" (single; LP Cheaper To Die out Nov 13)

For the hardcore/punk lane, Death Cassette shared "Clutter," the second single off their upcoming album Cheaper To Die, out November 13 via High End Denim Records. The Calgary/Western-Canada unit (an Exclaim!-familiar name around these parts) follow their 2023 EP Get Rid of It with a harder, more pointed set, and "Clutter" keeps that abrasive, straight-to-the-point punk bite. Status: Single out now; Cheaper To Die out Nov 13. Sources: Punknews.org (Oct 5).
